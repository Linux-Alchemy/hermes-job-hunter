# Proven cover-letter workflow pattern

## Evidence state

This is a sanitized procedural account derived from a successful private application workflow. It contains no private employer, candidate, résumé, contact, path, or application text.

## Inputs used

1. The user's raw notes about why the role and company mattered.
2. The employer-original posting.
3. A current company profile.
4. An approved résumé and evidence base.

## Reusable argument extraction

The successful process identified:

- a genuine opening hook specific to how the user encountered or understood the opportunity;
- the job's actual duty mix rather than relying on its title;
- two or three evidence bridges from the user's real background;
- one or two company details that proved research without reciting it;
- developing qualifications that needed precise wording;
- material research and risks that belonged in interview preparation rather than the letter.

## Collaboration sequence

1. The user created a shared workspace and supplied rough notes.
2. The specialist read all four input classes.
3. The specialist reflected the proposed letter spine and identified anything that should be softened or excluded.
4. The user approved the direction and requested a complete draft under the untouched notes.
5. The specialist appended one standard-format first pass.
6. The user reviewed the file directly and explicitly approved the wording.
7. The specialist created a clean Markdown source with notes and draft labels removed while preserving approved wording exactly.
8. Rendering and submission remained separate human-authorized steps.

## Why it worked

- The opening could only have come from that application.
- The user's real thoughts controlled the argument.
- Research supplied specificity without becoming a research dump.
- Approved evidence kept every claim honest.
- Posting language appeared only where it described actual work.
- The shared file reduced repeated typing and context switching.
- The process stopped when the letter worked instead of polishing it into generic recruiter prose.

## Reuse boundary

Reuse the four-source model, argument extraction, shared-workspace loop, evidence checks, and approval gates. Never reuse the successful application's sentences, motives, employer facts, or personal hook unless they are independently true and relevant to the new application.
